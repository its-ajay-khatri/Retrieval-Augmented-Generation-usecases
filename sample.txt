The Lamborghini Aventador was launched on 28 February 2011 at the Geneva Motor Show, five months after its unveiling in Sant'Agata Bolognese. Internally codenamed LB834, it succeeded the Murciélago as Lamborghini’s flagship model. Within months, 12 units were sold, and by March 2016, over 5,000 Aventadors had been built — surpassing its initial production plan of 4,000.

Aventador LP 700-4 (2011–2016)

The first version, LP 700-4, was designed by Filippo Perini. It featured a 6.5-litre V12 engine and a carbon-fibre monocoque chassis. The model’s production milestone of 5,000 units exceeded expectations; only eight moulds for the carbon monocoque were made, each designed for 500 uses.

Aventador Roadster (2012)

Announced on 27 December 2012, the Roadster shared the coupé’s V12 engine, accelerating 0–97 km/h (0–60 mph) in 2.9 seconds with a top speed of 349 km/h (217 mph). Its two carbon-fibre roof panels (6 kg each) could be stored in the front compartment. With added reinforcements, it weighed 1,625 kg, just 50 kg more than the coupé.

Aventador SuperVeloce (SV) (2015–2017)

Revealed at the 2015 Geneva Motor Show, the SV increased power to 750 PS (740 hp), reduced weight by 50 kg, and improved aerodynamics by 180%. It achieved 0–100 km/h (0–62 mph) in 2.8 s and exceeded 350 km/h. Production was limited to 600 units.
A SuperVeloce Roadster, unveiled at Pebble Beach 2015, weighed 1,575 kg and was limited to 500 units. Production ended in July 2017.

Aventador S (2017)

Introduced in December 2016 and shown at the 2017 Geneva Motor Show, the Aventador S featured four-wheel steering, revised aerodynamics, and LDVA active dynamics. Its 6.5-litre V12 produced 740 PS (730 hp) and 690 N·m (509 lb·ft), with 0–97 km/h in 2.9 s and a 350 km/h top speed. The S Roadster debuted at the 2017 Frankfurt Motor Show, 50 kg heavier due to chassis reinforcement.

Aventador SVJ (2018–2021)

The SVJ (Super Veloce Jota) debuted at Pebble Beach 2018 as a track-focused version with 770 PS (759 hp) and 720 N·m (531 lb·ft) from the reworked V12. Weighing 1,525 kg, it achieved 0–100 km/h in 2.8 s and 0–200 km/h in 8.6 s, topping 217 mph (350 km/h).
It introduced ALA (Aerodinamica Lamborghini Attiva) active aerodynamics and the LDVA 2.0 control system. Lamborghini test driver Marco Mapelli set a 6:44.97 Nürburgring lap record in 2018.
The SVJ Roadster, limited to 800 units, was unveiled at the 2019 Geneva Motor Show with identical performance.

Aventador LP 780-4 Ultimae (2021–2022)

The final Aventador, announced in July 2021, was limited to 600 units (350 coupés, 250 roadsters). It combined the SVJ’s mechanicals with a 780 PS (769 hp) engine, 720 N·m torque, and 0–100 km/h in 2.84 s, reaching 354 km/h (220 mph).
After 15 units were lost in the Felicity Ace ship fire (February 2022), Lamborghini reproduced them. The last Aventador, inspired by the 1968 Miura P400, was completed in July 2022 and auctioned with a unique NFT for over US$1.6 million.

Aventador J (2012, Concept)

The Aventador J, a one-off barchetta concept, debuted at the 2012 Geneva Motor Show. Powered by the standard 700 PS V12, it lacked a roof, windshield, air-conditioning, and radio for weight reduction (1,575 kg). Sold for US$2.8 million, its “J” name refers to Jota, a 1970s Miura one-off inspired by FIA Appendix J regulations.

Aventador LP 720-4 50º Anniversario (2013)

Marking Lamborghini’s 50th anniversary, this limited edition (200 units: 100 coupés, 100 roadsters) produced 720 PS (710 hp). It featured enhanced aerodynamics, a unique Giallo Maggio color, and exclusive interior trim. The coupé premiered at the 2013 Shanghai Motor Show, with the roadster shown at the 2013 Quail Motorsports Gathering.
